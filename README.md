# LLM value trade-offs

Do language models trade values off against each other consistently, like the monkeys in
Padoa-Schioppa's juice-choice experiments? And if they do, where in the network does the
trade-off happen? Research by Jasper Blank; code written with Claude.

| [`pilot7-value-route/`](pilot7-value-route/) | Pre-registered: where is option A's value formed before the decision stage? One value for A before the model reads B (the monkeys' "offer value")? Qwen3-14B and Llama 3.1 8B | Run 2 Nov. **No single value for A before B** in either model: at A's line end Qwen holds the good but not the number of people; Llama holds both, in different directions. After B, Qwen has a value-specific channel at the line breaks (layer 24) that carries good and number changes and moves oppositely for A and B: a comparison, not a price per option. | [`pilot8-intrinsic-values/`](pilot8-intrinsic-values/) | Pre-registered: how does the model trade off 24 intrinsic values (Clearer Thinking)? Does the same thing light up across trade-offs? | [`pilot9-vibe-check/`](pilot9-vibe-check/) | Pre-registered: if the model prices each sentence with one "vibe check", does asking it directly "How good would it be if …?" track the menu prices, including the wording quirks? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Partly shared.** The same wording wins within a value in 20 of 24 values (13 of the 15 where the rating differs at all), but overall r is only 0.55 (about 0.63 corrected for the ratings' noise): 54% of answers pile up at 7. The number of people moves the rating about a quarter as much as it moves choices |
| [`pilot10-full-range/`](pilot10-full-range/) | Pre-registered: with outcomes from trivial to life-changing (96 sentences, 45,600 trade-offs), does the direct "how good" rating track the menu? Was pilot 9's pile-up at 7 hiding agreement? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Sentence prices are stable across menus** (pilot 8's 48 sentences re-priced in a new menu: r 0.98; split-half 1.00) and driven more by the size of the outcome (63% of the spread) than by the value (20%), but still by wording: in 7 of 24 values a bigger outcome is priced below a smaller one. Over the full range the rating tracks the menu (r 0.81); among outcomes of similar size only r 0.5, also on a log scale (0.50), so the 7s were not hiding agreement |
| [`pilot11-vibe-direction/`](pilot11-vibe-direction/) | Pre-registered: is there one direction, read from the sentence itself, that predicts both the menu price and the stated rating, and that the model uses for both? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Readable but not shared.** One direction on the sentence's own words, before option B, reads the price held out (r 0.78; ridge 0.84), in both the menu and the rating prompt, but mostly as "big or small outcome". It does not carry the overlap with the rating (price and rating still correlate 0.65 without it). Pushing along it moves the choice by about 40% of the predicted amount at layers 16-20 (random: 0) and the rating by only 17% |
| [`pilot12-recipes/`](pilot12-recipes/) | Pre-registered: do price and stated rating share one variable ("one vibe"), or the same ingredients with different weights ("different recipes")? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Different recipes.** Where the rating disagrees with the price (beyond any smooth function of the price) can be read from the sentence as option A, before any rating question (held-out r 0.35, permutation p 0.01): moral and protective acts count more in the rating. Pushing along that disagreement moves the rating about 3.7 times more than its effect on the choice would carry; a random direction also departs from the shared-vibe line, but the other way. First run whose pod pushed its own results and stopped itself |
| [`pilot13-recipe-in-words/`](pilot13-recipe-in-words/) | Pre-registered: does the choice's recipe have a description in words? Ten descriptive features per outcome; do they predict the price, how do choice and stated rating weigh them, and do they price new outcomes? Does pilot 12's disagreement push beat 20 random directions? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **About 0.8, and no better than one question.** Nine features predict the price held out at r 0.79 (bar 0.8, fail) and 24 new outcomes at 0.71 (bar 0.7, pass), the same as asking "how big a difference would this make" or reading the model's states; about half of a new outcome's price stays with its exact wording. Qwen's own feature answers carry a halo (features correlate 0.46 with each other; it says outcomes it values right a moral wrong). Rated blind by Claude (exploratory), the features predict Qwen's price at least as well (0.84) and show moral acts and caring for others counting more in the stated rating than in the choice. The disagreement push beats all 20 random directions, narrowly; random pushes move the choice up to ±0.33 logits |
| [`pilot14-price-writers/`](pilot14-price-writers/) | Pre-registered: what writes the price? Which words of a sentence set it, which heads and MLP layers write it onto the sentence, and does the choice go blind to the price when they are silenced? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Many MLP layers a little each; the readout is not the whole price.** The layer-20 price readout is written by the MLPs of blocks 10-20 (4-7% each; MLPs 65%, heads 35%); silencing any one block (2-24) at the sentence does nothing, the 10 top MLPs together remove 32% (Q4's random control was broken by the design, so Q4 is unreadable). Single words move the choice a lot and by meaning: rescue words ("saved", "prevented", "cured") turn the harm they name into a good, "each" multiplies, "brief" or "mild" shrink. The readout barely follows these word-level changes (r 0.28; 0.10 for ordinary deletions), so the price the choice acts on is finished later or elsewhere |
| [`pilot15-price-route/`](pilot15-price-route/) | Pre-registered (no probability predictions): does Qwen value each option on its own before comparing? Does an option's worth funnel through one spot on its way to the answer? Is the comparison the generic "which is bigger" machine of pilots 5-6? | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **A rough value while reading, finished after both options, compared by a generic machine.** Before B is read, the best readout on A's own positions follows the choice's word-level changes at r 0.53 (bar 0.6), though it already gets big flips right; the line break after B at layers 20-24 holds a price that follows the words (0.85) and carries 43% of a swap of A's sentence alone (bar 0.5). At the answer position, pilot 5's 20 comparator pieces (the same ones that compare goods, piles of apples and weights) carry 60% of the swap (random ≤ 7%), and the decision arrives between layers 24 and 28. Pilot 14's Q4 redone: the price writers matter (32% against 5-6% for four random sets) but one set of mostly early MLPs matters as much |
| [`pilot16-relative-or-absolute/`](pilot16-relative-or-absolute/) | Pre-registered (bars only): is the finished price absolute, or relative to the alternative? 96 outcomes and 994 word deletions, each against 8 alternatives (8,720 menus) | Run 3 Nov, Qwen3-14B. **Partly relative.** About three quarters of an outcome's price is the same whatever it is compared with (common share 0.75 for single words, 0.76 for whole outcomes; bar 0.9), and the same holds inside at the line break after B (0.75), so the dependence is already in the finished price. It is not just saturation: 32% of clearly separated pairs of outcomes swap order between alternatives (27-36% in menus that are not near-certain), and the choices contain cycles no fixed values allow (film > dream job > an extra year of life > back pay > film) |

## How pre-registration works here

For pilots 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7, the design, the analysis code, the thresholds and both sets of predictions
(Jasper's and Claude's, Claude's sealed first) are committed before anyone sees a result. The format chosen in the tune phase is committed before the test phase. Results come
last. The commit history is the record.
